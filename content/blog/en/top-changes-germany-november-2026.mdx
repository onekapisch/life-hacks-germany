## Why November 2026 matters

The Federal Government’s overview, published on **October 29, 2026**, identifies several November changes that affect everyday borrowing, online orders, rail travel, and transport subscriptions. This is a practical summary, not individual legal or financial advice.

## 1) Consumer-credit protections now include small loans

From **November 1**, consumer-protection rules for credit agreements also cover small loans of up to **EUR 200**, including some "buy now, pay later" arrangements. The Federal Government also says that text form, for example an email or digital confirmation, can be enough to conclude a consumer-credit agreement; a handwritten paper signature is no longer always required.

Practical move: treat a digital confirmation for a small loan as a real credit commitment. Read the repayment schedule, total cost, and withdrawal information before accepting it, and keep the confirmation.

## 2) DB's family ticket is now a permanent offer

For travel from **November 1**, Deutsche Bahn offers its family ticket permanently. The ticket can cover one or two people aged 15 or older and up to four children aged **6–14** on a domestic long-distance return journey, with seat reservations for up to five people included.

At the time checked, DB lists these standard prices: **EUR 59.99** one-way long-distance or **EUR 99.99** for a long-distance return trip. Where a trip also includes local rail, DB lists **EUR 81.99** one-way or **EUR 143.99** return. The offer requires at least one adult and one child to travel together; check the live DB conditions before booking because route and cancellation conditions apply.

## 3) New handling charge for small parcels from third countries

From **November 1**, the Federal Government says a **EUR 2** customs handling charge applies to online-order parcels arriving from third countries with a value of up to **EUR 150**. The charge is calculated per customs commodity category within a parcel and is separate from the EUR 3 flat customs charge the Government says has applied since July 2026.

Practical move: before placing several low-value items in one order from outside the EU, check the seller’s checkout and delivery terms. The charge can depend on the composition of the parcel, not only its total basket value.

## 4) Deutschlandticket: wait for your provider’s instruction

The Deutschlandticket list price remains **EUR 63/month through December 2026** and rises to **EUR 66.80/month on January 1, 2027**. The Government’s November guidance clarifies the operational rule for existing subscriptions: if your transport company asks you to approve the continuation at the new price and the supplementary terms, act on that message. If it does not contact you, the subscription is adjusted or continued automatically at the new price.

Practical move: check the email address and in-app inbox linked to your ticket account before the end of December. Do not assume every provider uses the same consent flow.

## Action checklist

- Read small-loan and buy-now-pay-later confirmations as binding credit paperwork.
- Price the DB family ticket against your exact route and include the relevant children in the journey search.
- Check customs charges and seller terms for small parcels coming from outside the EU.
- Watch for a message from your Deutschlandticket provider before January 2027.
